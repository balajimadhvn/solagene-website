Structure & drug discovery

QSAR & Cheminformatics Modelling

We build ligand-based models that predict activity or properties from chemical structure, validated with cluster-aware cross-validation, Y-randomisation and an explicit applicability domain, and documented in a model card.

Request a free quote Learn to do this yourself

Who it is for

  • Medicinal-chemistry groups
  • Teams screening libraries before purchase or synthesis
  • Projects that need a defensible in-silico property model

What you send us

  • Measured activitiesSMILES with values and assay descriptions.
  • Or a targetFor curation of public data from ChEMBL.
  • Compounds to predictYour library or candidate list.

What you receive

  • Curated dataset
  • Validated model and performance report
  • Predictions with applicability-domain flags
  • Model card and code

Methodology

How the work runs

Each step is agreed with you and documented. Typical tools: RDKit · scikit-learn · XGBoost · ChEMBL · PubChem

  1. Data curationStructure standardisation, duplicate handling and harmonisation of activity types and units.
  2. Chemical spaceDescriptors, fingerprints, clustering and diversity analysis of the training data.
  3. ModellingRandom forest, kNN, gradient boosting or linear models, compared against simple baselines.
  4. ValidationCluster cross-validation, Y-randomisation and definition of the applicability domain.
  5. Prediction and reportPredictions with domain flags, a model card and the code to re-run them.

Quality controls

Checks applied to every project

  • Duplicates and conflicting measurements resolved and documented
  • Performance reported on cluster-based splits, not only random splits
  • Y-randomisation to rule out chance correlation
  • Every prediction flagged as inside or outside the applicability domain

Scope and limits

What the work can and cannot show

  • A model is only as reliable as its training data; small or noisy datasets give wide error bars, which we report.
  • Predictions outside the applicability domain are flagged rather than trusted.
  • Research use only: results are not a clinical or diagnostic report.
